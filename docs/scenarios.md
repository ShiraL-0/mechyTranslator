# mechyTranslator Scenarios
*Note: Some scenarios were drafter with the help from AI and edited by me (along with US)*

## Scenario 1
James is part of a rover club as a software member. He's is helping mechanical with repairing a wheel
during a competition. A teammate asks James to find him the gooseneck pliers. James does not know what 
that looks like. He tries to search it up on google however there is no Wi-Fi due to the amount of
people at the convention center. Instead, James remembers the app he had downloaded earlier. So he opens
the app and types "gooseneck ppliers." The app shows they're bent-nose pliers, with a picture. James
spots them in the toolbox and hands them over.

## Scenario 2
Maya is the team lead for mechanical of a rover club. During a competition, she's tightening a motor 
mount and her hands are covered in grease and she realizes she needs a T-handle. Her members are 
busy so she calls over another member from a different subteam. The team member has no idea what a 
T-handle is, so opens the app and holds it up while Maya says "T-handle" into the mic. The app shows it's
a T-handle hex key, with a picture. He finds it in the toolbox and hands it to Maya.

## Scenario 3
Mark is helping mechanical, and a teammate asks for a 1/2" wrench. Mark finds a wrench, but it's marked 13 mm. 
He isn't sure if it's close enough. He checks the app, which shows that a 13 mm and a 1/2" are close but not
the same, and using the wrong one could strip the bolt. Mark keeps looking and finds the right wrench.

## Scenario 4
After a long first day at competition, there are many loose bolts, screws, and washers scattered across the 
team's table. Desi offers to help sort them back into the right bins, but she can't tell them apart. She picks 
up a screw and takes a photo of it in the app. The app shows it's an M4 socket head cap screw and that it needs 
a 3 mm hex key. Desi sorts it into the M4 bin and keeps going, checking the app whenever she's unsure.

## Scenario 5
Priya is a new member of the rover club and is setting up the team's toolbox before a competition. She finds a tool 
she's never seen before and isn't sure what it's for. She takes a photo of it in the app. The app tells her it's a 
hex key and shows its other common name, Allen key. Now Priya knows what to call it when her teammates ask.

## Scenario 6
Lory is helping mechanical replace a damaged bracket on the rover. He needs to remove a bolt but doesn't know what size 
socket fits it. There's no Wi-Fi at the convention center. He opens the app and takes a couple of photos of the bolt 
from different angles. The app shows that he needs a 10 mm socket. Lory grabs it from the toolbox and gets to work.

## Scenario 7
The night before a competition, James opens the app at home on Wi-Fi. The app lets him know new tool data is available, 
so he downloads it.  The next day at the convention center, there's no internet, but the app still works with all the 
new tools included.

## Scenario 8
Leo is part of a robotics club. At the makerspace on Wi-Fi, he opens the app and searches "torque wrench." Along with 
the picture and description, the app shows a short video tutorial on what a torque wrench is and how to set and
use it properly. Leo watches it, then browses a few more tools he's seen in the club's toolbox. By the next meeting, 
he knows what most of them are and how to use them.

## Scenario 9
At the end of the competition, the team dumps all their loose hardware onto
a table to pack up. There are bolts, nuts, screws, and washers all mixed
together, and nobody wants to sort them one by one. Sam spreads them out and
takes a single photo of the whole pile in the app. The app labels each item
in the photo, like "M5 bolt," "M3 nut," and "1/4" washer," and groups them by
type. The team uses the list to sort everything into the right bins much faster.

## Scenario 10
Nina is working on the rover in a dim corner of the pit. She takes a photo of
a tool, but the app says it can't identify it clearly and suggests moving to
better lighting. Nina moves closer to a light and tries again. This time, the
app identifies it as a ratcheting wrench.

## Scenario 11
Omar is at the makerspace on Wi-Fi, planning to buy a set of hex keys for the
rover club. He searches "hex key" in the app and filters the results by brand
to compare options from companies the club already uses. He finds a set that
matches the club's other tools and adds it to the team's shopping list.

## Scenario 12
During a competition, Maya keeps needing the same few tools for repairs. Each
time she looks one up, she saves it in the app. Later, when a teammate asks
for one of them, she opens her saved tools and finds it in seconds instead of
searching again.

## Scenario 13
Maya, the mechanical lead, sets up a club library for her rover team in the
app. She adds the tools and hardware the team actually owns, takes photos of
each one, and notes where they're stored, like "red toolbox, drawer 2." Then
she invites the rest of the club to join. At the next competition, Ethan
searches "T-handle" and the app shows the club's own T-handle first, with
its photo and location. Ethan goes straight to drawer 2 and finds it.

# other related scenarios

## Scenario 14
Tyler is a first-year apprentice at an auto shop. His journeyman asks him to
grab a "knuckle buster," and Tyler doesn't want to admit he doesn't know what
that is. He quickly types it into the app, which shows it's a slang name for
a ratcheting wrench, with a picture. Tyler finds one on the tool wall and
brings it over without having to ask.

## Scenario 15
Ms. Chen teaches a high school shop class. Before the semester starts, she
sets up a class library in the app with all the tools in her shop and where
each one is stored. When students forget what a tool is called or where it
goes, they look it up in the app instead of asking her every time. This
gives her more time to help students with their projects.

## Scenario 16
Daniel is fixing a loose cabinet hinge at home. He doesn't know what kind of
screwdriver the screw needs, and it doesn't look like a normal Phillips head.
He takes a photo of the screw in the app, which shows it's a Torx screw and
that he needs a T20 driver. Daniel checks his bit set and finds the right one.

## Scenario 17
Rosa is restoring an older car in her garage. Some bolts on the car are
imperial while the newer replacement parts are metric. When a bolt won't fit
her 13 mm socket properly, she checks the app, which shows that a 1/2" socket
is the right match for that bolt. Rosa switches sockets and avoids rounding
off the bolt.

## Scenario 18
Ken volunteers at a community makerspace. Members often borrow tools and put
them back in the wrong spot. Ken sets up a makerspace library in the app with
each tool's photo and its storage location. Now, when members are done with a
tool, they search it in the app and return it to the right shelf.

## Scenario 19
Luis recently moved to Canada and started working at a construction site. His
English is good, but he doesn't know the names of many tools in English. When
his supervisor asks for a "pry bar," Luis searches it in the app, which shows
the name in both English and Spanish, along with a picture. Luis recognizes it
right away and grabs it.

## Scenario 20
Hannah works on a farm where cell service is unreliable in the fields. While
repairing a piece of equipment, she needs to figure out what size wrench fits
a bolt. She takes a photo of the bolt in the app, which works offline and
shows that she needs a 3/4" wrench. Hannah grabs it from the truck and keeps
working without driving back to the house.

## Scenario 21
After her grandfather passes away, Grace is sorting through his garage full
of tools. She doesn't recognize many of them and wants to decide which ones
to keep, donate, or give to family. She spreads a group of tools on the
workbench and takes a photo in the app. The app labels each tool, so Grace
can make a list and decide what to do with each one.

## Scenario 22
Alex's dining table has a wobbly leg, and one of the screws has come loose
and gone missing, so the table is now standing on three legs. Alex tries a
few screwdrivers from the junk drawer, but none of them fit the remaining
screws. He takes a photo of one of the screws in the app, which shows it's
a Robertson (square) screw and that he needs a #2 square driver. He also
takes a photo of the empty hole, and the app shows what kind of screw or
bolt he needs to replace the missing one. Alex picks up the right driver
and replacement screw from the hardware store and fixes the table.
